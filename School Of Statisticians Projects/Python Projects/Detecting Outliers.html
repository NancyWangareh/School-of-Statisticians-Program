9. What’s the impact of outliers on standard deviation vs IQR?

i. Compute both measures before and after outlier removal.

cat("\nStandard Deviation vs IQR:\n")
## 
## Standard Deviation vs IQR:
cat("Before:\n")
## Before:
cat(sprintf("  Age STD: %.2f, IQR: %.2f\n", sd(df$Age), iqr_age))
##   Age STD: 9.89, IQR: 14.00
cat(sprintf("  Income STD: %.2f, IQR: %.2f\n", sd(df$Income), iqr_income))
##   Income STD: 12099.93, IQR: 16446.50
cat("After:\n")
## After:
cat(sprintf("  Age STD: %.2f, IQR: %.2f\n", sd(zeroOutlier_Age$Age),
            IQR(zeroOutlier_Age$Age)))
##   Age STD: 9.65, IQR: 13.00
cat(sprintf("  Income STD: %.2f, IQR: %.2f\n", sd(zeroOutlier_Income$Income),
            IQR(zeroOutlier_Income$Income)))
##   Income STD: 11774.57, IQR: 16292.00

Impact of Outliers

🔹 Standard Deviation (STD)

  • Decreased noticeably in both Age and Income.
  • Why? STD includes all values and squares their distance from the mean, so outliers heavily inflate it.
  • The reduction implies that extreme values (outliers) were likely removed or corrected.

🔹 Interquartile Range (IQR)

  • Decreased slightly in both cases.
  • Why? IQR only uses the middle 50% of the data (between Q1 and Q3), so outliers don’t affect it much.
  • The small drop suggests that the core distribution stayed stable while only a few outer points changed.

Key Takeaway

  • Outliers inflate STD far more than they affect IQR.

  • In your data:

    • The larger drop in STD vs small drop in IQR confirms that outliers were present and are now reduced.
    • IQR stayed relatively stable, showing the central tendency of your data did not change much.

Conclusion

Outliers have a strong impact on standard deviation but minimal impact on IQR. That’s why IQR is often preferred for describing spread in skewed or non-normal data.
